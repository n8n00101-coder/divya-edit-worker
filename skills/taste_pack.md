# JULIE TASTE PACK v87 — the one taste every planning role reads in full

Distilled from DIVYA_EDITING_HANDBOOK.md, editing_playbook.md, the hark-kit
(DIVYA_VIDEO_BRAIN.md, EFFECTS_GUIDE.md), Blind's rules R1–R12 and the
v85/v86 diagnoses. This pack REPLACES truncated excerpts: if you are a
planning role (Story Analyst, Visual Director, Editing Director), this is
your whole taste. LLM = decisions; code = vetoes and execution.

## The one law
Every frame must have a reason, and the reason is the LINE being spoken at
that moment. A visual that cannot be traced to words in its own line is
wrong, however pretty it is. (Blind R2: the plan's "why" for a beat must
share actual words with the beat's line.)

## Meaning first (asset priority)
1. Real footage of the thing the line names (vision-verified, never
   claimed when absent).
2. A real document / data form when the line cites evidence or numbers
   (document, newspaper, chart) — the evidence FORM, not a stock icon.
3. Typography as the visual: a quoted sentence is typed out as text; a
   named term gets its own card; a spoken number is shown AS the number.
4. A drawn graphic — last resort, and only a metaphor for the line's
   meaning (battery for draining energy, scale for a weighed choice,
   clock for a deadline), never decoration.

## Format literacy (decided at intake, never an afterthought)
Format is a BRAIN decision, made when the job is first understood —
platform first, then canvas. Nobody should ever have to tell Julie
that a YouTube long video is landscape HD; that is table stakes, the
way a pro editor simply knows it. The user can override any default;
the user's SILENCE never does.
- YouTube long video → 16:9 landscape, 1920×1080 (Full HD). Never
  vertical, never SD — a portrait "long video" is a format defect,
  however good the edit inside it.
- YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels / TikTok → 9:16 vertical,
  1080×1920 (safe-zone law in the shorts section below).
- Instagram feed post → 4:5 (1080×1350) or 1:1 (1080×1080).
- No platform named → length decides: long takes the YouTube-long
  default, short takes the vertical default.
- HD floor: where the platform norm is HD, SD delivery is a defect,
  not a saving. A source below the delivery canvas is replaced or
  the beat is replanned — never upscale a soft source and ship it
  as if it were HD.
- One canvas per video: every chapter, title card and graphic is
  born at the job's canvas. A vertical chapter inside a landscape
  film (or the reverse) is a QC defect, not a style.
- Never letterbox or pillarbox a deliverable to fake a format —
  bars mean the format decision was wrong. Reframe the shot
  (crop-and-follow) or replan the beat.
- The format (platform, canvas, orientation) is written into the
  plan BEFORE the first beat is planned. If a later instruction
  changes the platform, the format is re-decided first and every
  planned beat is re-checked against the new canvas.

## Beat grammar
- One sentence = one beat. The picture changes every ~2–5 s; hook beats
  are shortest (2–4 s), evidence may be held, the payoff and the closing
  beat are allowed to breathe.
- The first 2 seconds must contain a visual EVENT (not a title fading in).
- The last visual must match the closing line's own words.
- No two consecutive beats share a visual, a section background, or a
  background kind. No template more than 3 times in a video; a deliberate
  motif is chosen in the plan, not stumbled into.
- A comparison visual ONLY for a real contrast the line itself sets up
  (two opposing ideas weighed). A rhetorical "yet" earns nothing.
- A number graphic ONLY for a number the line actually speaks. A stamp
  ONLY with a word taken from the line — canned defaults ("BANNED") are
  a defect, never content.
- An enumeration is laid out as its items, in the line's own words.
- Labels and headlines are the line's own words — never production
  internals, never a word from a different line.

## Effects discipline (the Compromise failure: the same round graphic in
~40% of frames — variety is PLANNED here, never audited into existence)
- Every effect must MEAN something; if the voice does not justify it,
  delete it. One effect at a time; calm between hits.
- One hit per sentence: the visual lands ~80 ms before the spoken word,
  the sound lands on the word.
- Enter fast (0.25–0.45 s), exit faster, hold long enough to read (≥1 s).
  Ease everything; nothing linear except glitch and typewriter.
- Transitions mark IDEA changes only. Inside one idea: hard cut.
  Transition length 0.3–0.45 s, on a sentence boundary.
- Restraint is the Editing Director's main job: removing an effect is a
  success, not a loss.

## Frame composition
- The visual must FILL its band — a small island centred on an empty
  frame is a failed frame (compliance A1), however correct its content.
- Safe area: ~60 px sides; headline top ~25%, visual middle, captions in
  the bottom band only; max 3 colours + white; high contrast; no text on
  busy areas; max 2 fonts (condensed bold for headlines).
- Slow push-in (2–4%) or drift on calm scenes so nothing is ever dead
  static — but a held payoff frame is a decision, not a freeze defect.

## Sound (planned AND executed — a curve nobody renders is a lie)
- Music intensity follows the story arc: hook 0.7, context/evidence 0.5,
  contradiction 0.55, turning point dips to 0.3, payoff lifts to 0.85,
  conclusion decays to 0.4. Music always ducks under narration and lifts
  only in non-narration windows.
- The delivered loudness range (LRA) must MOVE — a flat ~2.5 LU line
  means the curve was never executed (v85 AND v86 shipped exactly that).
- Silence is a tool with exactly three sanctioned spots: after the
  turning point, just before the payoff, just before the ending
  (0.8–1.5 s). Silence anywhere else is a defect.
- SFX belong on story beats (emphasis words, turning points), not on
  every boundary by rule. A whoosh on every cut is noise, not design.
- Final loudness −14 LUFS ±1.5, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.

## Story shape (documentary)
- One documentary = one opening, one ending, one CTA at the very end.
  Chapters are movements, not shorts: no chapter grows its own hook,
  ending or end card (the Compromise video had 7 endings).
- Arc roles come from the SCRIPT's turning points, not from chapter
  position arithmetic: hook → central question → context → evidence →
  contradictions → emotional development → turning point → build →
  payoff → conclusion.
- The contrast line gets a real contrast; the quote line is set as a
  quote; the evidence line is presented as evidence. Form follows the
  line's rhetoric.

## Honesty contract
- Never claim footage, a source, or a check that does not exist. A check
  that could not run is UNVERIFIED, never PASS.
- A failed beat is re-PLANNED (a new visual decision), never merely
  re-rendered — re-rendering the same plan reproduces the same failure.
- Every applied change by the Editing Director is written as a diff.
  Authority is auditable or it does not exist.

# ADDITIONS — Premiere Pro reel masterclass (merged 2026-11-15)
Distilled from a 6-reel editing course; every rule below was checked against the laws above — where the course conflicted with this pack, the pack won.

## Listen before you plan (the course's first law, all six reels)
- No beat is planned from text alone. The planning pass listens to the
  voice and leaves every line with a named visual idea. (Reels 3 & 6:
  "listen 2–3 times until every line is imagined.")
- Flag the lines a viewer cannot grasp from words alone — those beats
  MUST explain visually, not decorate. (Reel 6 planning pass.)
- Cut every silence and retake before any design decision. Design on a
  dirty timeline is wasted design. (Reels 2 & 6 rough cut.)

## Composition before animation
- Build the finished still frame first; animate only an approved frame.
  A beat whose still frame does not work will not be saved by motion.
  (Reels 3 & 4: "the whole game is composition.")
- The background is never dead: a slow drift, a subtle texture motion,
  a breathing pattern. Static is a decision (held payoff), never a
  default. (Reel 1 hook drift; Reel 5 living background.)
- Blend, don't paste: a cutout's edges are feathered into its world and
  its base may fade out entirely — the viewer must never be left asking
  what is below or behind an element. (Reel 3 officers on paper.)
- A fake shadow grounds every floating element; when the element
  changes size, its shadow changes with it, or the lie shows. (Reels
  4 & 5.)
- Depth pass before delivery: grade the whole frame, blur what the eye
  should leave, sharpen where it should land, vignette or falloff so
  the frame has a focus point. A flat frame is an unfinished frame.
  (Reel 6 ending pass.)

## Colour is meaning
- Headlines start ALL WHITE. Colour is then given only to the words
  that earn it. (Reel 2.)
- Assign colour by the emotion the line must cause: money green,
  danger/threat red, the aspiration word highlighted yellow, a salary
  figure red on purpose to make the viewer uneasy. If you cannot name
  the emotion, the colour is decoration — remove it. (Reel 2.)
- The key phrase changes colour exactly ON its spoken word, not before
  the sentence and not after it. (Reel 1: the fear line turns red.)
- For a brand/product topic, sample the palette from the product's own
  packaging and world before inventing colours. (Reel 5: orange from
  the box, brown map to harmonise.)
- A topic's colour family is declared in the plan (finance = green)
  and held for the whole video. (Reel 6.)

## Show it in its real form — or build the metaphor
- Evidence appears AS its real form: the document glows as a document,
  the article keeps its masthead, and the camera travels INTO the row
  or line that matters. Never describe evidence the frame could show.
  (Reel 1.)
- A familiar interface is shown as-is — recognition is the point and
  the viewer reads it instantly. (Reel 6 stock screenshots.)
- An abstract claim gets a BUILT metaphor, animated: profits vs returns
  as a balance whose gap is filled level, a ratio as a seesaw tipping
  on the spoken <, =, >. A metaphor that moves with the words beats a
  screenshot that merely sits there. (Reels 3 & 6.)
- Figure count carries meaning: one figure = one man; three figures =
  an institution, the leader centre-front. Compose the semantics, not
  just the scene. (Reel 3.)
- A spoken enumeration is laid out as a visible list, in the line's
  order, in the line's words. (Reel 5: the two reasons, two boxes.)

## Numbers and anchors
- A number the argument depends on is PINNED small on screen until the
  argument is done — viewers forget a number the moment it leaves.
  (Reel 2: the on-road price never leaves.)
- Repeated data in one video uses ONE declared template (parameter +
  label + amount), duplicated and re-typed. Consistency of data
  presentation is professionalism, not template laziness. (Reel 2.)
- A key term may MORPH: type it, erase it, retype it as its short form
  (2510 → 25i) so the viewer watches the term being born. (Reel 2.)

## Transitions are a vocabulary
- Scene change → light leak or wipe. Topic change → pan. Emphasis →
  zoom punch-in with motion blur. Era change → pan AND a new world kit
  (paper, grain, vignette travel together). (Reels 4 & 5.)
- A transition with no meaning is deleted; inside one idea, hard cut.
  (Reel 4, stated outright — matches the pack's existing law.)

## Sound placement (the course's taxonomy, Julie's discipline)
- Three layers, three jobs: foley makes it real, ambience makes
  silence alive (even silence has noise), music makes it felt. A beat
  missing its layer is a plan defect. (Sound Design section.)
- Match the effect to the EVENT's physics: whoosh only on real
  movement, pop on a sudden size change, riser into a reveal, bell on
  good news, hit on the emphasis word, small hard ticks as objects
  land one by one. (Sound Design section; Reels 1 & 3.)
- The per-sound test, asked at planning time: "what difference does
  this sound make to the viewer?" No answer → no sound. A substitute
  sound that fulfils the same need is acceptable. (Reel 2.)
- If the scene's own sound already does the job, add nothing.
  (Sound Design section.)
- Placement is creative and per-event; LOUDNESS is a separate batch
  pass over all placed effects at the end. Never mix the two jobs.
  (Sound Design section.)
- Music is briefed by MOOD words drawn from the story arc ("suspense,
  mysterious, not dark"), never by genre name alone. (Sound Design
  section.)

## Moves with names (preset thinking)
- Every reusable move declares its anchor: timed from the beat's start,
  from its end, or proportional to its length — chosen when the move is
  defined, not improvised per beat. (Presets section.)
- Text that rises is masked at its baseline so it EMERGES from the
  line it sits on; a slide without the mask reads as a sticker.
  (Presets section: Curve Border's hidden second job.)
- A glow is a shadow with no direction: one recipe, reused everywhere,
  so every element lives under the same light. (Presets section;
  Reel 1's single shadow recipe.)
- Motion follows arcs, not straight lines, and lands slightly uneven —
  perfectly even motion reads robotic. Overshoot belongs in pops
  (0 → ~75% → 100%). (Reels 3 & 6.)
- When a planned effect cannot be rendered, re-route the beat to the
  nearest achievable equivalent and LOG the substitution in the plan.
  The idea is never dropped silently. (Presets section: the
  missing-effect workaround, generalised.)

## Restraint (the minimal reel's lesson)
- Shadow is given "just enough to be felt." The over-shadowed version
  is shown in the course as the cheap-looking one. When in doubt,
  halve it. (Reel 4.)
- A small colour change that fixes contrast is worth more than a new
  element. Before adding anything to a weak frame, try changing what
  is already there. (Reel 4: the brown title.)
- Minimal is a style chosen per topic, with the same rigour as maximal
  — grain, vignette and one symbol watermark can carry a whole world.
  (Reel 4.)

# — Professional editors' canon (merged 2026-11-15)

## Deciding between two legal options
- When two cut points, visuals or orders are both legal, choose by
  Murch's order — emotion > story > rhythm > eye-trace > screen plane
  > spatial continuity — and record which criterion won in the plan.
- A technically imperfect option that serves the emotion beats a clean
  option that kills it. Continuity is the cheapest criterion (4%) —
  sacrifice it first (never across a backbone veto: 180°, eyeline).
- Every beat's plan entry carries its intended FEELING (1–2 words)
  next to its "why". No named feeling = no defence for the visual.

## Phrasing (the level above the beat)
- Plan in phrases of 3–6 beats: one tension build, one release. Big
  rhythm changes happen at phrase boundaries, not on a metronome.
- QC checks three rhythms separately: physical (something moves),
  emotional (the feeling travels), event (the story advances). A
  phrase that fails event rhythm is cut, however pretty.
- A preparation and its payoff are never separated by an unrelated
  beat. If beat N winds up, beat N+1 lands it.
- Beat length follows the content curve: a beat ends when its
  information is absorbed. Dense beats get full read time; simple
  beats are cut the moment they land.
- Inside a phrase the rhythm stays consistent — a rhythm break is a
  signal, reserved for the turning point.

## Empathy and story economy
- Scenes with people or a persona open with the want-test in the
  plan: who wants what, why right now, what if they don't get it.
  No nameable want → restructure or cut the scene.
- When a line lands, hold the reaction for its measured length.
  Time the reaction first; trim everything else around it.
- Story-economy pass on every script: delete each line that adds no
  information, feeling or turn; let the next beat carry the moment.
- A monologue of facts is visualised on the receiving side — the
  face, the consequence, the person affected. The source appears
  for the key line only.
- Every plan ends with a subtraction pass: list every effect and
  device; remove each whose removal loses nothing. The removals
  are written into the diff like additions.

## Documentary passes (structure before polish)
- Paper edit before visuals, always: spine paragraph, beat list,
  order — as text. No visual is chosen before the paper edit exists.
- String-out test: the lines in order, no visuals, must hold as a
  story. If they don't, fix the script — never cover a broken story
  with visuals.
- Passes in order, never merged: assembly (all beats, loose) →
  structure (rearrange, cut, shape the arc) → visuals (cover the
  locked structure) → sound (curve and SFX on locked picture).
- The structure pass names its deletions (beat + reason) in the
  diff. A structure pass that deleted nothing did not happen.
- The spine states a CHANGE or a QUESTION, never a topic: what is
  different at the end, or what the ending answers.
- Post-render, QC screens as an audience member and logs the beats
  where attention drops. Those beats are re-planned first.

## Retention engineering
- Match the promise: the hook's implied promise is visibly confirmed
  within the first 2 beats. The first cliff is a broken promise.
- Stakes by beat two: why this matters to the viewer (cost, risk,
  gain, curiosity debt). Topic alone is not stakes.
- Tease the destination in the first quarter: a glimpse of the
  payoff's shape, never the payoff itself.
- Staircase, don't plateau: each reveal bigger, stranger or more
  consequential than the last. A smaller beat after a bigger one is
  swapped or merged.
- Re-engagement beats are marked in the plan before rendering:
  ~40–50% in a short; ~1/3 and ~2/3 in a longer piece. A stakes
  raise, a reveal, a new element, a direct question.
- The governor: never cut faster than the story can carry. A beat
  the emotion needs held is held. Pace serves story.
- Cause and effect stay in the same beat: claim + proof visual,
  number + meaning, setup + payoff image are never split by a cut.
- Land the key point twice — fact, then consequence — as two
  different visual ideas. Two good hits read as one great hit;
  a repeated template is not a second hit.
- The payoff must be earned: its setup (question, stakes, partial
  answer) exists earlier in the plan, or the payoff is demoted.
- (ASPIRATIONAL — needs the analytics feed) Log drop-off seconds
  per published video in editing memory. The same structural cliff
  in 3 videos becomes a standing fix in the next plan; a spike's
  structure becomes a reusable pattern.

## Montage, with the honesty leash
- Juxtaposition only where the line itself makes the comparison —
  the viewer builds the contrast the words already set up.
- A cut may never imply a fact, cause or source the line does not
  state. If the montage claims it, the line must say it.

## Sound decisions
- At an idea change, choose the lead: sound-first (~0.2–0.4 s
  pre-lap) to pull the viewer in, picture-first for a reveal.
  Never default; the choice is in the plan.
- A hit is earned by the calm before it. Widen the calm; don't
  raise the hit.
- Planned silence is near-silence: room tone or music tail stays
  under it. Digital zero reads as a failure, not as drama.

# ADDITIONS — Trending documentary craft (merged 2026-11-16)
Distilled from frame studies and editor breakdowns of the documentary
channels that define YouTube now (Harris, Vox, fern, LEMMiNO, Nexpo,
Magnates Media, ColdFusion) plus measured pacing/retention research.
Full knowledge: JULIE_BRAIN_KNOWLEDGE_DOCUMENTARY_TRENDING.md. Every rule
below was checked against the laws above — where trending practice
conflicted with this pack, the pack won (the conflicts are logged in the
knowledge file, §6).

## Evidence is an apparatus, not a decoration
- Every documentary ships a source log: every visual, in on-screen
  order, with its publication/owner, its date, and where it is held.
  Every borrowed clip is logged before render — source, timestamp in
  the original, seconds used, the claim it supports. 100% or no render.
  (LEMMiNO's published source pages; essay-craft clip logs.)
- One source-card style for the whole video: verbatim quote, then
  author + title + year in a smaller line; page shots keep the
  publication name and date visible in frame. (Essay craft.)
- Highlight in sync with the read: on any document or screenshot, dim
  everything except the sentence being read; the highlight wipes across
  the phrase as the voice reads it (onset within ±0.5 s of the word).
  Never show it pre-drawn. (LEMMiNO; Vox suppression emphasis.)
- Play the real recording before interpreting it: where a real
  recording exists, ≥3 s of it runs with no narration over it; the
  interpretation comes in the next beat. (Nexpo; LEMMiNO.)
- Match picture to claim, not to paragraph: a new specific claim gets a
  new claim-matched shot, even mid-paragraph. Generic stock is
  forbidden under a specific named person, place, number or event —
  every b-roll asset is scored for specificity against the beat's own
  words before it may be used. (ColdFusion; Harris.)
- Label every reconstruction for 100% of its frames (RECONSTRUCTION /
  ILLUSTRATION), styled distinctly from archive. Era texture (grain,
  VHS) only on assets whose real date matches that era — never age
  modern footage. In reconstructions, imply violence by composition
  and light; never depict the act. (Genre disclosure practice, fenced
  by the honesty contract.)
- Correct on the record: a wrong image found after publish gets a
  description correction naming the timestamp, what was shown, and
  what should have been shown. (ColdFusion.)

## Documentary structure at trending standard
- State the mystery by 0:60: no intro, sponsor or promise-speech before
  it. The first narration sentence gives a precise date + place, or
  plays over real archival audio of the event. (LEMMiNO.)
- Build long videos as 6–8 mini-arcs: each has its own visual setup,
  one complication, one resolution — and re-establishes its board in
  its first 60 s, so a mid-video joiner can follow. (Harris; fern.)
- Evidence first, context second, on a 30–90 s micro-cycle: show the
  concrete visual, withhold the explanation, pay it off inside the
  cycle. Abstract narration never runs >10 s without a visible anchor.
  (Harris.)
- Adjudicate theories one at a time, in one template: claim → evidence
  for → evidence against → status (supported / unsupported /
  unresolved). Never montage competing theories together. (LEMMiNO.)
- Update a timeline graphic at every date jump, and give every location
  change a map beat (label the new place, draw the route from the
  previous one). A date or place the narration moves past without its
  graphic is a plan defect. (LEMMiNO; true-crime craft.)
- Documentary narration runs at 140–165 wpm, in historical present for
  story beats; past tense only once the outcome is known. A 30 s window
  outside the pace band is flagged at the script gate. (Harris; fern.)
- End on the landing: payoff → human aftermath → one wider question.
  The final beat is the thesis or an explicit statement of what is
  still unknown; the single end CTA follows the landing and nothing
  follows the CTA. Where the opening visual can return stripped of all
  labels and overlays for the close, use it. (LEMMiNO; fern; Harris.)

## Loops and re-hooks, run as a ledger
- Keep a loop ledger in the plan: at most 3 loops open at once — 1 main
  (the title's question), 1 chapter, 1 scene. A new loop opens every
  60–90 s in long-form; closes run at roughly 1 per 2 opens. No moment
  before the final 10% has zero loops open; the main loop closes inside
  the final 10%. An abandoned loop is a broken promise, logged like one.
- Close-and-open in the same breath at every chapter join: the last
  seconds of a chapter open the next chapter's question. No chapter
  ends with all loops closed. Chapter transitions are mini cold opens,
  never recaps.
- Three timed re-hook gates, on top of the 1/3 and 2/3 beats: a drift
  interrupt at 0:25–0:35 (framing change, music drop or SFX); a second
  gate at 0:50–1:30 (reframe the promise — new urgency, a twist, doubled
  stakes); a 10–20 s re-hook at 40% of runtime ±2% (restate why the
  viewer is watching, raise the stakes, change location, framing,
  graphic style or music). Candidate re-hooks are marked every ~2 min
  in the outline; only earned ones (new question, stake or payoff)
  survive. Never 3 minutes without one.
- Place the reframe turn at 60–75%: one explicit sentence that recasts
  everything shown so far as a larger claim — "what happened" becomes
  "what it means". Absent, before 50%, or after 80% fails the plan.
- Never announce the ending: no wrap-up phrases, no summary close. The
  ending phase is the slowest-cut phase of the film — the payoff needs
  room to land.

## Pace by phase, measured in refresh events
- Cutting rate follows the runtime curve, not a flat rate: hook fastest,
  setup fast, body breathing (median 4–5 s), climax re-tightens,
  resolution slowest. The closing phase never cuts as fast as the hook.
- A refresh is wider than a cut: push-in, overlay, text reveal, graphic
  animation, state change inside a held graphic, SFX-anchored frame.
  Inside one idea the picture may hold while refreshes carry the pace —
  a held map or graphic must change state at least every ~3 s, and no
  held graphic goes >5 s with no state change.
- Deliberate holds are budgeted, not accidental: 2–7 holds longer than
  10 s per 10-minute block, placed at emotional or evidentiary peaks —
  and a hold earns its length only if something inside it keeps moving
  (push, parallax, draw-on, state change). A fully static frame obeys
  the normal beat ceiling. A block with zero long holds and zero short
  shots fails the variety check.
- Cut on the voice turn, not the music grid: picture changes land on
  narration turns (new sentence, name, place). Music may polish a cut's
  timing; it is never the grid. Protect the breath: the narrator's
  pause before a reveal is storytelling — never trim a beat before it
  has landed, and give each chapter's biggest statement ~1 s before
  cutting away.

## Maps and visual explanation (the Harris/Vox grammar)
- A map beat starts wide (continent/country) and pushes to the named
  place inside the line that names it. Strip the base map's labels; add
  back only the places the narration names, each on its naming beat.
  Never hold a static full map while places are named off-screen.
- Keep one map spine per geography segment: footage plays as an inset
  on the map instead of cutting away from it, and map sequences toggle
  to ground level — never maps alone for more than ~60 s. Where aerial
  footage exists, put the map labels on the aerial shot.
- Badge every named actor and keep the badge resident until that
  actor's role ends (≤7 co-resident). Each side of a conflict keeps a
  fixed screen side for the whole video; a meaning change (allied →
  fighting) is a dot/colour change on the badge, not a new graphic.
- Instantiate counted nouns as counted objects: a spoken number ≤8 of a
  thing is shown as that many pins/icons/objects, not written as a
  digit. When the narration points ("here", "this city", "down
  there"), the camera move starts with or before the pointing word.
- Run every beat in arrival order: evidence enters first, the gesture
  (highlight, circle, arrow) draws second on the detail as it is named,
  the takeaway type lands last. One focal object per beat at 50–70% of
  frame; everything else is demoted. Exactly one gesture per beat, and
  exactly one element in the frame wears the accent colour — the one
  the narration is on.
- The quote-lift for sourced quotes: show the real document, push into
  the passage, highlight it, then lift the quote as set type with the
  document ghosted behind and attribution in its fixed small line.
  Never typeset a quote whose source document has not been shown.
- Visualise the mechanism, then play the example: for an invisible
  process, build the simplest diagram first, add one element per beat,
  and play the real example at the exact beat the diagram completes.
- Every narration line names its visual referent in the plan — the
  on-screen object it talks about. A line with no referent fails the
  script gate.
- Punch in on the word, to the region: emphasis zooms target the row,
  phrase or face (never frame centre), land settled exactly on the
  naming word, ease in and out, and release to neutral before the next
  transition. Cut transitions at peak velocity, motion direction
  matched, the sentence bridging the seam. Never cut to blank between
  graphics: two graphics separated by <10 frames merge into one
  continuous graphic.
- Evidence is a physical object: photos and documents carry a hard
  short shadow and at most 3° of tilt; screenshots keep their UI
  chrome; nothing floats flat and shadowless. A person still used as a
  graphic is cut out as a paper piece — silhouette, off-white border,
  soft shadow — never a rectangular photo pasted on a graphic ground.
- Backgrounds for graphic beats are built from tinted subject imagery
  (blacks lifted to dark grey, texture over the top), never a flat
  solid. One texture treatment covers every graphic in the video.

## Music is edited, not laid (the cue-sheet system)
- Spot the music in the script: a cue sheet lists every cue's in/out,
  its role, and its intended end (natural ending / dropout /
  crossfade). One unbroken track under a whole film is a plan defect.
- Cut music only on phrase boundaries (downbeats, 4- or 8-bar units);
  loop whole phrases verbatim, never arbitrary seconds. The final
  musical accent lands after the final narration word.
- Use stems before volume: duck only the stem that fights the voice,
  restore it in no-voice sections. Carve a static −2 to −3 dB pocket
  at 1–4 kHz in the music bus under narration — level ducking alone
  still masks consonants. No lyrics under speech, ever: a vocal
  version plays only where narration is absent for its full duration.
- Tag every dropout: key-statement, evidence/natural-sound,
  testimony, or chapter-break full stop. A chapter-break full stop
  (music + SFX + voice stop together ≥0.8 s on room tone, restart =
  one hard sound or one voice entry) counts as that movement's
  sanctioned silence. Drop the music ~1 s before each chapter's
  biggest reveal.
- Budget design accents: risers, braams, impacts, stingers ≤4 per
  minute in narration sections, never two braams within 30 s. Every
  riser ≥2 s is paid off within 0.5 s of its peak — impact, reveal
  cut, or deliberate dropout. Accents land only on scripted story
  markers, never on consecutive beats.
- One ambience bed per location, unbroken under every cut inside that
  location, changing only on a location change. Solo test: clearly
  audible alone, barely noticeable in the mix.
- J/L cuts are the default join: at a change of location or time,
  offset the audio 0.3–1.5 s unless the join is tagged a hard chapter
  break. A film whose joins are all straight in picture and sound
  reads chopped.
- Attach tactile foley to every graphic entrance, matched to the
  object: paper for documents/photos, marker for highlights,
  click/tick for counters and pins, snap for cutouts. One sound per
  entrance; a silent graphic entrance is a plan defect. Give each
  chapter one sound motif, used only at that chapter's transitions
  and tension beats.
- Keep the voice flat when the content peaks: on the darkest or most
  shocking lines, VO level stays within ±1 dB and pace within ±10% of
  the video median. Music and SFX carry the spike; the narrator's
  volume never does.
- Voice chain, fixed order: clean → high-pass 80–100 Hz → presence EQ
  → compress ~3:1 → de-ess if sibilant. Compression before cleaning is
  a defect. TTS pauses are built by function — comma, sentence and
  scene-change pauses at audibly different lengths; uniform pauses
  are a synthetic tell.

## Finish: matching, type roles, honest charts
- Correct before grade, in order: normalise → exposure/white balance
  → match neighbours → creative grade last. Declare one reference shot
  per sequence and match to it in the order exposure → white balance →
  contrast → saturation → texture. Run the three-cut test (previous →
  current → next) across every source-type change.
- The texture pass is separate from the grade: reconcile grain and
  sharpness afterwards, softening the too-perfect source — never
  sharpen archive up to stock. Protect accuracy colours: flags,
  charts, scientific scales, UI and product colours are never
  re-graded to fit the look.
- Assign font roles, not just fonts: Font A = Julie's own voice
  (statements, stats, labels; never tilted, always on grid); Font B =
  quoted/archival voice; dates, sources and coordinates in mono or
  Font A's smaller weight, in one fixed position. Set type shows the
  operative phrase only (~4–6 words), never the full narrated sentence
  — verbatim text belongs in captions.
- Numbers are protagonists, sources are furniture: every number shown
  large carries its source in small type directly beneath it, in the
  same position on every chart and number card in the video.
- Charts are honest by construction: bar/area axes start at zero, no
  exceptions; a line chart may start above zero only with its axis
  values legible for its whole duration; every chart discloses its
  time window, its unit/denominator, and "log scale" if logarithmic;
  dual axes and 3D charts are banned. A chart's title may state only
  what its data directly shows. Charts build in argument order (axes →
  data → annotation, the data landing within 0.5 s of the narration
  naming it), carry no legend and no filled panel — series are
  direct-labelled at their ends, the featured series in accent, the
  rest grey — and a chart that changes basis morphs into its next
  state instead of cutting to a second chart.
- Captions and cards are different jobs: captions are verbatim,
  full-line, chunked at clause boundaries; kinetic type is emphasis
  only — ≤1 accent word per card, placed in open frame space, never
  over a face, never the only text track in a documentary section.
  Every kinetic word is timed from the waveform (±0.15 s of the spoken
  onset), not from a transcript guess.

## Vertical is its own frame (shorts/reels)

- One subject per beat in 9:16 — one face, one product, one line.
  Context comes from the next beat, never from widening this one.
- Safe zone is law: all critical content (faces, hook text,
  captions, numbers, the product) inside the centre 900×1400 of
  1080×1920. Dead: top ~120 px, bottom ~320 px, right ~120 px.
- Every short is four gates, in order: HOOK (0–2 s, opens a loop)
  → CONFIRMATION (2–5 s, proof the promise is real — never skip)
  → PAYOFF (in steps, one idea per beat, the hook's question
  answered only at its step) → LOOP or one CTA. A slow sign-off
  ending is banned.
- Pattern interrupt every 2–3 s, rotating in kind (cut → sound →
  graphic → punch-in); an interrupt must carry information, not
  just motion. B-roll inserts run 0.8–2.0 s unless the insert is
  the payoff step.
- Loop only genuinely re-watchable content: frame loop (last
  frame = frame 0 in composition/position/colour) or sentence
  loop (last line feeds the hook line). Hooks are written to be
  heard twice — no greetings, no "in this video".
- Muted-first design: the story must read from captions + picture
  alone; 2–5 words on screen, clause-chunked, house karaoke sync.
- Landscape into vertical: crop-and-follow the action. Blur-pad
  is banned (standing law, restated because it recurs).

## Tutorials must be followable

- Structure: DESTINATION (finished result shown first — it is
  the hook) → PATH (steps in the viewer's performance order) →
  PROOF (the same finished state, working, at the end).
- One action per beat, signalled before it happens (line, step
  number, or highlight lands before the cursor moves). Two
  actions in one beat is a defect.
- Step furniture in one fixed position (Step n of N); the same
  step list is the chapters. Prerequisites are step 0 and are
  shown, not assumed.
- The cursor is the actor: intentional moves, arrive-pause-act;
  wandering/idle cursor is dead air and is cut. Zoom/highlight
  lands BEFORE the click on every small target. Clicks and
  keystrokes carry subtle sound.
- Typing is shown as first-chars → cut → completed field, full
  text legible in the held frame. Waits/loads cut to zero or to
  a LABELLED time-skip. Each phase boundary gets one recap beat
  showing the accumulated state.
- A step is never hidden and an on-screen failure is never
  silently jumped: show the honest fix or re-record the step.

## Colour: correct, then grade, then protect

- Order is fixed: BALANCE (per-shot black/white points, cast
  neutralised, scopes over eyes) → MATCH (every shot to its
  scene's reference shot, one reference per scene) → GRADE (one
  named look) → PROTECT (accuracy colours).
- A look must be nameable in one phrase per video ("cold
  institutional", "golden archive"). GRADE_LOCK applies it
  identically to every beat; the look never changes mid-video —
  only the story's light may.
- Palette discipline: 2–3 dominant hues + 1 accent; saturation is
  spent on the accent, held back elsewhere.
- Skin is protected: face hue stays on the skin-tone line and
  saturates less than accents, whatever the look does elsewhere.
  A grade that breaks skin is rejected, not shipped.
- Accuracy colours (flags, charts, scales, UI, product colours)
  are never re-graded to fit the look.
- A LUT/preset is a starting point applied AFTER correction, at
  partial strength — never the grade itself, never on raw mixed
  footage. Mixed sources are normalised individually to the
  reference: soften the too-perfect down, never sharpen archive up.
- Delivery check at phone size/brightness; shadow floor stays a
  few points above zero so blacks survive platform compression.
  Consistency across shots beats beauty in any one shot.

## Sound design: layers, palette, stacks

- Four layers, fixed priority, mixed from the voice outward:
  VOICE (levelled first, house constant level) → IMPORTANT SFX →
  MUSIC (automated, carved 6–10 dB under speech, lifted in gaps)
  → AMBIENCE (the floor). Every sound names its job in ≤3 words
  or comes out.
- Build a palette per project BEFORE cutting: 1–2 ambiences, 3–5
  foley basics, 5–10 spot effects, the music cues — each function
  with 2–3 variants, rotated. The same effect twice in a row is
  a defect. Sources ₹0-legal only (Freesound CC0/CC-BY with
  attribution recorded, own synthesis, house library).
- Foley sells reality: sync to the exact frame of contact,
  built in micro-layers (contact + surface + cloth), timing and
  velocity varied between repetitions, felt more than heard
  (~8–12 dB under voice) unless foley is the beat's subject.
- Ambience is laid before detailed editing; place changes are
  crossfaded, not hard-cut, unless shock is intended. A dropped
  floor (designed silence) is budgeted like a payoff — rare,
  placed, purposeful.
- Spot effects are STACKS, never single files: whoosh = low
  sweep + mid body + high air (+ peak click); impact = sub thump
  + mid crack + high detail + short tail. Placement: sound LEADS
  picture — transition sounds start ~2–3 frames before their
  cut; a riser peaks exactly at the reveal frame.
- Riser → impact → silence is the standard tension unit, spent
  at act turns and payoffs only. One designed spot event per
  beat remains the ceiling; foley/ambience are layers, not spots.
- Mix check on a phone speaker: every sub-heavy stack carries a
  mid/high layer that survives it. Contrast is pacing — a track
  at one intensity throughout is a defect.

## Trailers: sell the feeling, resolve nothing

- A trailer is a standalone short film selling premise + tone +
  one central question. It never summarises and NEVER resolves
  anything; the work's best moment is withheld or shown out of
  context, not spent.
- Three movements, shot lengths descending: SETUP (~25% — world
  + character, 2–4 s shots, sparse music, premise in dialogue) →
  TURN & ESCALATION (~45% — inciting event on a smash cut or
  silence drop, score changes gear, stakes named, 2–3 fragment
  title cards) → CLIMAX + BUTTON (~30% — biggest images on the
  music's hits, final hit on the strongest image, title card,
  then the BUTTON: one last joke/scare/line, built to be
  repeated to a friend).
- Build order is the houses': log out-of-context lines + images
  → one-sentence premise + one-sentence mood → MUSIC CHOSEN AND
  HIT-MAPPED → radio cut (must play with eyes closed) → picture
  laid to sound → sound design (risers, braams, the silence drop
  before the biggest hit) → cards and button last.
- Dialogue is sequenced, never re-worded: lines may be reordered
  to build the argument; they may never be spliced into claims
  nobody made. No fake review quotes, ever. Tone may be
  emphasised, never misrepresented.
- Teasers sell mood + one image ("what is this?"); trailers sell
  the question ("what happens?"). Every Julie long video gets a
  teaser cut in this grammar (vertical version obeys the shorts
  gates on top).

## Beat craft: the track leads

- The finished mix is pinned first and locked; the section map
  (intro/verse/pre-chorus/chorus/drop/bridge) and the beat grid
  (real BPM; sec/beat = 60/BPM) exist before any shot is chosen.
- Cut on BARS, not beats. Rate ladder: verses 2–4 bars/shot →
  builds 1–2 bars → chorus/drop 1 bar to 1 beat → sub-beat only
  in bursts of ≤8 beats. Signature move: hold through the verse,
  halve shot length exactly on the chorus downbeat.
- Mix the three cut types in every piece: hard beat cuts
  (downbeat), melodic cuts (phrase/flourish changes), action cuts
  (the picture's own movement peak). A cut 1–2 frames BEFORE the
  beat is the pro adjustment when a passage feels late.
- The hero shot is placed on the biggest musical moment from the
  section map, before assembly — never left to leftovers. Shot
  content energy must match section energy, not just shot length.
- Visible performance must sync (syllable, note, gesture) — sync
  first, beauty second. Deliberate holds across a grid line are
  emphasis, budgeted like designed silences.
- Murch's order outranks the grid: emotion > story > rhythm. A
  cut on the snare that breaks the feeling is a bad cut.

## Comedy: clarity, holds, escalation

- The gag must be READABLE: wide, static, clear frames holding
  setup → action → consequence together; close-ups are for
  reactions, never to hide the action. A cut that removes the
  consequence removes the joke.
- Timing lives in three holds: the anticipation hold before the
  gag, the gag at full clarity (never trimmed for pace), the
  reaction hold after — ending when the laugh would peak, not
  when it ends. First assembly instinct is too short: reaction
  holds run ~1.5–2× instinct. Holds are written into the beat
  sheet at planning; a comedy beat sheet without holds is
  unfinished.
- Wright grammar: action-movie treatment (whip pans, crash
  zooms, heroic synced sound) applied to genuinely mundane
  action — the contrast is the joke, and both sides must be
  fully committed. Build a cutting rhythm of three matching
  beats, then BREAK it: the break is the punchline.
- SFX are the dialogue track in no-dialogue comedy: oversized,
  frame-synced, stacked; sudden silence after a barrage is the
  other punchline. Captions may set up a gag; they never
  annotate the visible ("he fell down!" is banned).
- Repetition is structure only with escalation: plant → return
  bigger → third return pays off or breaks the pattern. Running
  gags are ledgered like open loops; a planted gag that never
  pays off is a defect. The shorts pattern-interrupt budget does
  not apply inside a running gag.

## Narrative scenes: radio first, cut on the thought

- Build order is fixed: RADIO EDIT (audio only, must play
  eyes-closed) → coverage assembly → reaction pass → trim.
  No pass undoes an earlier pass without naming why.
- Cut on the THOUGHT, not the line-end (the blink point).
  Dragnet ping-pong — whole line per speaker-shot — is
  banned as a default; it survives only as chosen flatness.
- Every major line gets a planned listener beat; move the
  picture to the listener wherever a line's IMPACT outranks
  its delivery. Reactions are never trimmed to "keep pace".
- Split edits are the default grammar: J-cut (next audio
  leads picture by ~4–12 frames) and L-cut (audio carries
  over the reaction). A dialogue scene with zero split
  edits is unfinished.
- Murch's Rule of Six is the priority, never traded
  downward: EMOTION (51%) > STORY (23%) > RHYTHM (10%) >
  EYE-TRACE (7%) > 2D PLANE (5%) > 3D SPACE (4%). Sacrifice
  from the bottom up; diagnose a wrong cut top-down.
- 180°/eyeline check runs on every scene: axis drawn, one
  side only (cross only via on-screen move or neutral shot),
  A looks frame-right / B frame-left in every shot, gaze on
  the partner's eyes — never at camera.
- Inserts only for story objects the audience must read;
  return from an insert to the exact eyeline state the
  scene left.

## Horror / true crime: dread is a wait with a reason

- Every dread beat names: what the audience knows, what the
  character doesn't, what is withheld. Slow with no
  anticipated threat is a defect, not atmosphere.
- Plant the odd detail first; delay its meaning by beats;
  reveal the implication only after the lean-in. A loop
  opened must close — withholding that never pays off is
  bait and is banned.
- Cutting baseline is flat and long (holds past comfort,
  negative space the eye must search); break the baseline,
  never speed it — one sharp cut or silence against
  flatness out-screams a barrage.
- The only scare shape: SETTLE (quiet baseline) → FALSE
  RELEASE (guard drops, hold 1–2 beats past comfort) → HIT
  (one event, one sound spike) → aftermath hold. True hits
  are rare and never telegraphed by the same swell twice.
- Sound leads picture: J-cut the dread sound under the
  innocent shot. One wrong detail per scene in the undertone
  bed; designed silence (~2–4 s before a hit or hardest
  fact) is budgeted like a payoff.
- True-crime apparatus is built once and reused identically:
  timeline/map cards held ≥6–8 s, one fact isolated per
  card, verified/alleged/unknown visually distinguished,
  reconstructions labelled on screen every time.
- True-crime restraint: measured narration, silence around
  the hardest facts, NO stinger on a death/victim beat, no
  music swell under the victim's section.

## Ads: one message, one ask, hold the brand

- One message (one sentence, written before scripting) and
  exactly ONE CTA in exact words, said AND shown, as the
  last spoken thing. A beat serving a second message is cut.
- Word budget is arithmetic: ~2.5 words/sec — 30 s ≈ 70
  words max, 15 s ≈ 35, 6 s ≈ 12–15. Over-budget scripts
  are cut on paper, never read faster.
- 30 s structure, in order: HOOK (0–3 s, brand by 5 s) →
  PROBLEM (3–8 s, product unnamed) → PRODUCT-AS-TURN
  (8–12 s, big and close) → HOW IT HELPS (12–22 s, product
  DOING the job, ≤7 words per card) → PROOF (22–26 s, one
  real item) → CTA + ENDCARD (26–30 s).
- 15 s is cut FROM the 30 (problem+product share a shot,
  proof becomes one text line); a 6 s bumper is three shots
  only: product close → one benefit in 3–5 words → logo+CTA.
- Endcard is a designed still held long enough to read:
  ≥~3 s in a 30, ≥2 s in a 15. End on the product, never the
  presenter; nothing else competes on the card.
- Scripts are two-column (picture | sound, timings summing
  to the spot). If the picture column alone doesn't tell
  the story, the spot fails muted — rewrite the picture.
- UGC inverts the surface only (phone framing, presenter
  first, spoken-confession hook); the five-beat skeleton is
  identical, and the ad still ends on the packshot.

## News packaging: grammar without impersonation

- Package shape is fixed: ANCHOR INTRO (who/what/where in
  sentence one; never the conclusion) → REPORT (strongest
  actuality first, facts in causal order, one SOT, nat-sound
  pockets, latest development last-but-one) → TAG (what's
  next / still unknown; never repeats the intro).
- Every element has a slug, a duration and a running order
  before assembly; total running time is checked arithmetic.
  Package / VO / VO-SOT grammars are never mixed mid-item.
- Lower third = who + what qualifies them, two lines max,
  shown ONCE per person at first appearance, held to the
  read-twice floor (~4–5 s), text never changes mid-hold.
- One furniture budget: bug + ONE graphic at any instant.
  Bug is single, small, static; ticker runs at one constant
  speed, never carries the current item's facts, and drops
  out entirely under a solemn/victim beat.
- Graphics exist only for facts pictures can't show
  (numbers, timelines, maps, exact quotes); one fact per
  card, built in the order narration speaks it, source
  credited on the card, charts on honest scales.
- Quote cards are verbatim with speaker + context; a trim
  that changes meaning is fabrication.
- Labels state only what's true: no BREAKING/LIVE furniture
  on non-live material, no real channel's name/look/bug —
  Julie borrows the grammar as her own explainer format.

## Vlogs: cut the breath, plant the payoff

- Cut every breath, hesitation and filler: silences >~0.3 s
  come out. Cuts land on breath and THOUGHT boundaries —
  mid-thought jumps are glitches, thought-boundary jumps
  are pace. Leave a breath after a punchline so it lands.
- Punch in or out at jump cuts (medium ↔ tighter crop;
  source 4K, deliver 1080p). Identical-framing jumps every
  2 s read as nervous, not energetic.
- The day's want, obstacle and payoff moment are planted in
  the first 30 s and revisited every 2–3 min. A segment in
  which nothing changes is cut to a montage beat or removed.
- B-roll is punctuation: it covers jump cuts, lands ON the
  noun it illustrates, runs only while that thought runs
  (1–4 s), and returns to the face for the reaction. The
  face never vanishes long enough to make a slideshow.
- Talking-head fatigue is timed: an interrupt inside every
  ~12–15 s of static frame (2–4 s per visual change in
  short-form), rotating in kind — cut → graphic → b-roll →
  sound.
- Long-form rhythm: start tight (change every 10–20 s),
  widen once hooked (25–40 s), and break calm stretches
  every 2–3 min with a 5–10-cut burst. The oscillation,
  not the speed, sustains 8+ minutes.
- Cold-open on the day's best real moment (visual event in
  2 s), then rewind — the payoff reached later must be that
  same moment, honestly arrived at.

## Weddings / events: words are the spine, one summit

- Emotion over chronology, always: open on the day's single
  strongest moment (a vow line, the first-look reaction),
  never on drone + title card. The archive edit keeps the
  order; the highlight keeps the feelings.
- Selects first: pull the lines and the breaking-voice /
  room-goes-quiet moments before cutting. Five strong
  moments carry a 4-min film; cutting from rushes produces
  chronology by default.
- Speeches and vows are the spine. Keep WHOLE SENTENCES and
  the speech's own structure (intro → rise → punchline →
  toast); a cut never lands mid-word or mid-sentence.
- Music bridges the gaps BETWEEN speeches and never covers
  words; words are mixed first. Drop the music (~2 s of
  quiet, or out entirely) before a vow or a major speech.
- ONE biggest moment only: the most emotional track goes to
  the vows OR the first dance, never three competing peaks.
  Every other beat is graded below the summit.
- Keep pockets of natural sound (laughter, applause, the
  room's hush) under montage — sync sound is what makes it
  THIS wedding, not a stock video.
- Event grammar transfers: a corporate/keynote film's vows
  are its keynote lines — whole sentences, same music and
  summit discipline.

## Animation: the animatic is the cut

- The film is edited FIRST: shot order, count, lengths and
  cut points are locked at ANIMATIC. If it doesn't play in
  boards, it doesn't go to production — polish never fixes
  timing.
- The animatic is finished in structure, deliberately
  unfinished in craft; judge it only on one-look readability,
  breathing timing and the turn landing — never prettiness.
- Soundtrack first: voice (final where possible) and temp
  music/SFX are recorded BEFORE boards are timed; panels are
  cut TO the track. Silent-animatic approval for dialogue or
  comedy work approves a different film.
- The X-sheet converts the lock to frame numbers; animators
  execute it. A timing question found in production goes
  BACK to the animatic and is re-locked there, once, for all.
- Frames are spent on acting: holds for anticipation and
  reaction, extra frames where a decision must be read;
  steal frames from travel/mechanics, spend them on faces.
- Blocking-first: pose stills must read in one look (who
  does what to what) before animation; the 180°/eyeline
  check runs on the LAYOUT, inside the one locked scene
  file per location — never after render.
- Post is conformance, not discovery: finished shots splice
  into the living Leica reel at the SAME duration and order;
  a shot arriving off-length is a defect sent back, never
  absorbed by re-trimming neighbours.

## Kinetic type: hierarchy first, speed is arithmetic

- Every card has ONE named role — VOICE (the narration's own
  words, never paraphrased), QUOTE (verbatim + attributed,
  visually distinct) or DATA (facts, tabular style, sourced
  on the card) — and the same role keeps the same treatment
  all film.
- Lock the static card first (face, sizes, weights, position)
  and read it frozen; motion never rescues bad typography.
  One primary line + at most one supporting line per card.
- Exactly ONE accent word per card — the word the beat turns
  on, chosen at script stage. Two accents = no accent.
- Entrance grammar is a fixed house set per role (mask/wipe,
  blur-in, punch on the accent, word stagger); total reveal
  ≤~800 ms on ease-out curves, exits quieter than entrances.
  A type card may BE the transition at act turns only.
- Reading limits are hard: hold ≥~0.3 s/word + ~1 s minimum
  per card after reveal; voice phrases ≤2–3 words/sec; hook
  cards ~90 wpm; DATA/QUOTE cards slow to 40–60 wpm.
- Kinetic words land on their spoken onset within ±0.15 s;
  QC checks the accent words first. Phrases chunk at 2–5
  words on clause boundaries — an over-budget script loses
  WORDS, never gains speed.
- Type never fights the face: no card over a face, type sits
  in negative space / lower-third / full-frame cards, and
  face + dense type are sequenced, never simultaneous. The
  layer runs OVER real footage — full-frame type only at
  hooks, act turns and data beats, in short runs.
